The Agency Quotient

A measure from my book, The Student Founder, of how quickly you act on an idea.

By Andrew Ashur, founder and CEO of Lucid Bots · November 19, 2026

Short answer

Your Agency Quotient is the time between “I should” and “I am.” Count the days between having an idea and doing the first thing about it that a stranger could react to. Smaller is better, and the best count is zero.

How to count it

Say that on a Tuesday in September you notice nobody in your dorm can tell whether a washing machine is free without carrying a load down three floors. On Saturday you tape a sheet of paper to the laundry room wall with a pen on a string, where people write their name and when they’ll be back. Four days passed between the idea and something a stranger could react to, so your count is four.

If you taped the sheet up on Tuesday, on the way back upstairs, your count is zero. If you’re still describing the laundry room to your roommate at Thanksgiving, it’s seventy and still rising.

Research and planning don’t count, because nobody can react to them. A sheet of paper on a wall counts, because a stranger can react to it.

Why it matters

It’s the one number in the book that doesn’t depend on your bank account or who you know. Most of the companies I sell to decide in quarters and ship in years. At Lucid Bots we try to work in hours and days, and we picked up that habit in a dorm.

It’s a count you keep yourself, not a tested psychological scale. You take it at the start of the book and again on Day 30 of the sprint, and you watch whether it falls.

Take yours

Think of the last idea you had that nobody assigned you. How many days did it sit before you did the first thing about it that a stranger could react to?

days

Reading about it doesn’t count. If the idea is still sitting there, count from the day you had it to today.
